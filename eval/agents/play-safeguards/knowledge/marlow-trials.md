# Marlow trials of safeguards in community raffles, 2021 to 2024

Eleven field trials in community raffles and prize draws. "At risk" means a household that spends more than 2%
of its monthly income on the game.

## Safeguards

A cap of 4 tickets per household per draw. At-risk households fell from 6.1% to 2.4% of players. Revenue fell by
7%. The single most effective measure per euro lost. A cap of 10 changed nothing measurable.

A monthly spending limit the household sets itself at its first purchase. At-risk households fell from 6.1% to
4.3%. Revenue fell by 2%. Works only if the limit cannot be raised within the same month.

Cooling-off. A player can bar themselves for 3 draws with one click. Used by 1.8% of players; two thirds of them
were in the at-risk group.

Donation framing. "Give 5 EUR to the playground; three givers win a prize" against "Win 2,000 EUR". Under the
donation framing, buying again right after a loss (chasing) fell by a third and sales were unchanged.

## Features that do harm

Near-miss messages ("you were two numbers away"). Spending in the next draw rose by 14%, and reported regret
tripled. Leave them out.

Frequent draws. With weekly draws the at-risk share was twice that of monthly draws.

One big prize. Winner-takes-all draws had 40% more chasing than draws with several prizes.

One-click repeat buying and saved payment details. Repeat purchases within an hour of a loss were 2.5 times as
frequent as with a fresh purchase each time.

Showing a player's running total of "almost wins" or a leaderboard of buyers raised spending among at-risk
households only.

## What did not matter

Warning texts on the ticket. The colour and name of the game. Age of the player, among adults.

## Who is at risk

Households in the lowest income fifth were 3 times as likely to be at risk as the rest. Players who bought on
the day after a loss were 4 times as likely.
