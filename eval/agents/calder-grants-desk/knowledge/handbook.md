# Calder Fund: grants desk handbook

## What the fund pays for

Calder Fund gives grants to teams building on NEAR. A grant pays for work that ends in something others can use: a product, a library, a tool, a public report. It is not an investment and the fund takes no tokens or equity.
The fund has three tiers, named Seed, Build and Scale. There are no other tiers, and the desk does not make exceptions to the ceilings.

## Tiers and ceilings

Seed: up to 15,000 USDC, for a prototype or a study by a team of any size, including one person.
Build: up to 60,000 USDC, for a product that will be live on mainnet at the end of the grant.
Scale: up to 180,000 USDC, for a live product with users that needs to grow; only teams that finished a Build grant or show equal traction may apply.

## How a grant is paid

Seed is paid in two parts: 50% when the agreement is signed and 50% on delivery.
Build is paid in three milestones of 30%, 40% and 30%. The first is paid at signing, the second when the product runs on testnet with the agreed features, the third when it is live on mainnet.
Scale is paid quarterly against the metrics agreed in advance; a quarter that misses its metrics by more than a fifth is paid at half.
All payments are in USDC on NEAR, to an account the team controls with a multisig for Build and Scale.

## How long a decision takes

Seed: a decision within 12 business days of a complete application.
Build: within 25 business days, which includes one call with the team.
Scale: decided by the committee, which meets on the first Thursday of each month; an application must be complete 10 business days before the meeting to be on its agenda.
The application window for each month closes on the 20th; what arrives later is read in the next month.

## What an applicant must provide

Every tier: a description of the work, a budget by line, the team and what each person did before.
Build and Scale: the code must be open source under MIT or Apache-2.0. A team may keep a hosted service around it closed.
Identity checks are required for every team member who owns 10% or more of the project. A company must exist before the second milestone of a Build grant is paid; Seed can be paid to individuals.
Anything that will hold more than 100,000 USDC of users' funds needs a security review by an auditor from the fund's approved list before the last payment.

## Scoring

Each application is scored by two reviewers on five criteria with fixed weights: team 25, technical plan 30, fit with the ecosystem 20, traction 15, realism of the budget 10. The scores are averaged.
The pass mark is 68 out of 100. An application under 68 gets written feedback and may be sent again after 60 days.
When the two reviewers differ by more than 15 points, a third reviewer scores it and the two closest scores count.

## Matching for work with approved partners

A grantee that has a signed integration with an approved infrastructure partner gets a match of up to 25% on top of the grant, paid with the last payment. The match exists because integrations with core infrastructure are what later projects build on.
The approved list currently has six partners; it includes Orrin Bridge and Halden Stake. The list is reviewed twice a year.
The match is one per grant, not one per partner: integrating two approved partners still gives at most 25%.

## Audit support

For Build and Scale grantees the fund reimburses 40% of the cost of a security audit, up to 12,000 USDC, when the audit is done by an auditor from the approved list. The reimbursement is paid on the auditor's final report, not on the invoice.
Seed grantees get no audit support.

## What is not funded

Token launches and anything whose main deliverable is a token sale. Gambling. Work that is a copy of an existing project with less than 30% of its own code. Marketing with no product behind it.
The fund does not pay for work done before the agreement was signed.

## Reports and what happens when they are late

A grantee sends a milestone report within 10 days after each milestone date. A report more than 21 days late pauses the grant; a paused grant is closed after 60 days without a report.
If a project stops within 6 months of its last payment, the unspent part of the grant is returned.

## Two cases the desk keeps as examples

Case CF-114 (2026-03): a Build grantee asked for the match for two partner integrations at once. The desk paid one match of 25%, as the rule says, and the rule was rewritten to say "one per grant" in plain words.
Case CF-131 (2026-07): a team applied for Scale without a finished Build grant, showing 9,000 monthly users. The committee accepted the traction as equal and set the first quarter's metric at 12,000.
