# Sorvia: Community Games Act 2021, practice notes

Art. 3. Scope. A game of chance is any scheme in which a stake is paid for a chance at a prize. A community game
is one organised by a non-profit body (association, cooperative, parish, school) for a stated public-benefit
purpose, with tickets sold only to its own members or residents. Everything below is about community games.

Art. 5. Notification or licence. Ticket sales of up to 90,000 EUR in a calendar year: the organiser notifies the
municipal registry at least 25 days before the first draw, naming the purpose, the ticket price, the prize plan
and the person responsible. Above 90,000 EUR a year: a licence from the National Games Board, decided within 90
days of a complete application, fee 1,200 EUR. Splitting one game into several to stay under the threshold is
counted as one game.

Art. 6. Ticket price. At most 10 EUR per ticket.

Art. 7. Prizes. At least 45% of the ticket sales of each draw is paid out as prizes of that same draw. Prizes in
kind count at their purchase price.

Art. 8. Costs. The organiser's costs of running the game may take at most 12% of ticket sales. Everything that is
neither prize nor cost goes to the stated purpose. Nobody may be paid a share of sales: a commission, or a fee
that grows with sales, is unlawful whatever it is called.

Art. 9. Each purchase is its own act. A ticket is bought by a separate, explicit act of the buyer for one named
draw. Standing orders, subscriptions and automatic renewals are prohibited.

Art. 10. Minors. A person under 18 may neither buy a ticket nor receive a prize. The organiser checks age at the
sale. A prize that falls on a ticket held by a minor goes to the stated purpose.

Art. 11. Withdrawal. A buyer may withdraw until sales for the draw close, and gets the full ticket price back
within 14 days. A game that does not let buyers withdraw may not be run.

Art. 12. Limit per person. No person may hold more than 20 tickets in one draw. The organiser may set a lower
limit, per person or per household.

Art. 14. The draw. A draw is either held in public, where members may attend, or made by a computer program whose
random value comes from a source that neither the organiser nor any participant can influence or predict; the
method is published before sales open.

Art. 15. Publication. Within 7 days of each draw the organiser tells all participants: tickets sold, sales,
prizes paid, costs, and the amount that went to the purpose.

Art. 16. Claiming a prize. A winner has 60 days to claim. An unclaimed prize goes to the stated purpose. The
organiser may not make payment depend on a step that it can withhold.

Art. 18. Who may not take part. The persons who conduct the draw, and the members of their households.

Art. 21. Records are kept for 5 years.

Art. 23. Penalties. A fine of up to 20,000 EUR or three times the amount unlawfully kept, whichever is higher. The
Games Board may bar an organiser from running games for 3 years.

Tax. A prize of up to 2,500 EUR is free of tax for the winner. Above that the organiser withholds 15%.
