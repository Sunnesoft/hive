# Nine Estates Study, 2022 to 2024

Nine housing cooperatives, 700 to 2,400 households, each raising money among its own residents for a shared
project. Six ran raffles, three ran appeals. Sources: 310 interviews, participation records, complaint logs.

## Who takes part

Owners and tenants. For a long-lived asset (a playground, a roof garden) 52% of owner households took part and
24% of tenant households. Tenants said: "we may be gone before it is built". Where tenants got a seat in the
group that chose the project, their participation rose to 39%.

A vote on the purpose. Estates that let residents vote on what the money is for, before the first draw, had
participation 9 percentage points higher than estates where the board chose.

Newcomers. Households that moved in within the last two years took part at half the rate of the others, unless
a neighbour invited them in person.

## What breeds suspicion

Winners close to the board. In 3 of the 6 raffle estates a board member's relative won a main prize in the first
year, and each time a dispute followed. Estates that barred board members and their households from taking part
logged 70% fewer complaints.

The money trail. Publishing the full accounts after every draw (tickets, sales, prizes, costs, what went to the
project) raised repeat participation from 31% to 44% of households.

A visible milestone. A sign on the site with the sum raised so far kept participation steady; without it,
participation fell after the fourth month.

## What divides

Public lists of buyers. Where the list of who bought tickets was posted in the stairwell, participation of
low-income households fell by 18 percentage points, and non-buyers reported being "looked at". No estate that
kept purchases private saw this.

Door-to-door selling by board members was felt as pressure by 4 households in 10; a stand in the courtyard was
not.

Pressure on those who do not play. In two estates residents who never bought a ticket were left out of the
opening event. Both estates recorded lasting conflict. Estates that invited everyone, buyers or not, did not.

## What kept trust

The count of each draw done by two residents who are not on the board, chosen by lot. A fixed end date for the
raffle, announced at the start. A named person to complain to, with an answer within two weeks.
